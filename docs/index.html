Built around how people here eat

Star ratings won't tell you which saloon still has its bullet holes or which soda fountain Travel Wyoming calls the state's oldest. Cowboy State Eats is organized the way people here eat: historic saloons, steakhouses and cookouts, small-town cafés, soda fountains and farm-to-table kitchens, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source or an official tourism listing. Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out on the open road.

What stays on your phone

Cowboy State Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Cowboy State Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The saloon, steakhouse, café, soda fountain, farm-to-table, pizza, hotel dining and oldest-places lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a dated 2025 or 2026 Travel Wyoming article or Travel Wyoming's own listing behind each place. The wider directory of 1,422 restaurants starts from open map listings (Overture Maps), filtered to the kinds that proved reliable where states publish restaurant lists.

Where can I find an old saloon?

Our historic saloons list has 9 hand-checked bars from Travel Wyoming's June 2026 guide, from Miners and Stockmen's in Hartville, which it calls the state's oldest bar, to the Million Dollar Cowboy Bar in Jackson. In the web search, the list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do I check a Wyoming restaurant's inspection?

Wyoming's Department of Agriculture inspects restaurants wherever no local health department does; the state posts no letter grades and publishes no results as data, and the reports sit behind a search form. So the app shows no inspection results: each place links to the department's inspection reports page, and we compute no grade.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

No. Wyoming's liquor licenses are issued by each city and county, and no statewide licensee list is published, so the app doesn't say either way.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
